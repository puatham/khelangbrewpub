# Hop science and hopping

## Lot-aware decisions

When available, use actual package/COA/lot information for alpha acids and product characteristics. Same-variety lots can differ significantly.

For current YCH products, lot lookup can expose alpha/beta acids, total oil, HSI, oil components, sensory data and, for some lots, survivable-compound measurements.

## Bittering

Use a defined calculation model and actual alpha acid. Tinseth estimates are models; laboratory IBU is measurement.

## Whirlpool / dry hop

Evaluate:
- temperature and contact time;
- product form;
- hop load;
- yeast state;
- oxygen exposure;
- polyphenol/vegetal load;
- beer losses;
- expected aroma return;
- hop-creep risk.

Do not convert “high kräusen dry hop” or any other timing into a universal rule.

## Hop creep

Brewers Association Hop Creep Technical Brief (2026) identifies three conditions relevant to hop creep: residual unfermentable real extract, live yeast in suspension, and hops added to fermenting/fermented beer. Consequences can include changes in alcohol, diacetyl and CO2; post-package over-pressure is a safety concern.

If gravity resumes after dry hopping, do not package simply because the beer previously reached target FG.
