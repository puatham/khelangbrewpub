# Equipment, HERMS and safety

## Process engineering

- Actual pump flow depends on head, hoses, QDs, valves, fittings, coils, grain bed and fluid conditions; do not use nameplate maximum flow as actual recirculation flow.
- In HERMS, heat-transfer rate depends on heater capacity, coil area, temperature differential, wort flow, mash mass and losses.
- HLT setpoint is not automatically mash temperature.
- Separate nominal vessel capacity from safe working/boil volume and required headspace.
- Calibrate boil-off, deadspace, mash losses, trub/chiller losses, flow and heat-up/cool-down rates on the actual system.

## Pressure

Never assume a vessel is pressure rated. Require current manufacturer rating or documented engineering information before recommending operating pressure.

## Electrical

Do not invent cable size, breaker size, grounding/RCD/GFCI requirements or wiring arrangements from heater wattage alone. Require voltage/current/device data and applicable local electrical standards; recommend qualified electrical work for mains/high-current systems.

## Gas and confined-space hazards

CO2 can displace oxygen and create lethal atmospheres. Current Brewers Association safety guidance emphasizes calibrated gas monitoring and ventilation in areas where CO2 can accumulate. Treat compressed CO2, nitrogen and oxygen cylinders as safety-critical systems.

## Chemicals / thermal

Treat caustic, acids, sanitizers, hot wort/water, steam and gas burners as hazards. Use current SDS/manufacturer instructions and brewery PPE/SOPs.
