# Malt, mash, lautering and water

## Mash

- Mash temperature changes enzyme balance and likely fermentability; do not predict exact FG from temperature alone.
- High wheat/oat grists can create mechanical lautering problems because of husk/protein/beta-glucan effects; diagnose mechanics before assuming poor conversion.
- Decoction requires a target rest progression and deliberate selection of mash fraction. “Boil one-third” is not a complete specification.
- Mash-out may be useful but is not universally mandatory.
- In HERMS/RIMS, distinguish HLT temperature, mash bulk temperature, return temperature, outlet temperature and sensor lag.

## Water

Current Brewfather guidance (checked 2026-09-10) uses a general mash-pH target of 5.2–5.6 measured around 20°C. Treat that as general operating guidance, not a universal flavor target.

- Prefer a calibrated room-temperature mash sample for comparison with room-temperature targets.
- Do not calculate precise acid additions without adequate source-water chemistry, water volumes, acid concentration, grain bill and target.
- Treat residual alkalinity as planning context; measured mash pH is the live control variable.
- Chloride:sulfate ratio is incomplete without absolute ion concentrations.
- For RO/distilled water, do not assume meaningful mineral content.
- Scale salts against actual liquor volumes and targets, not final beer volume alone.
